How it fits together

Three surfaces, one session

The phone app drives. The TV runs the launcher, which frames each game's own TV page. Every device in the room is a member of one couch session; the person who cast is the host.

Phone app

  • Your OGS profile and friends
  • TV tab: Cast once, then it's the remote
  • Games open full screen; swipe from the left edge to leave; Rejoin returns to the same room

TV launcher

  • Icon row; the focused game's art fills the room
  • Cards: your latest sitting, Surprise me, the next sitting
  • The game grows out of its box; Home shrinks it back

Games

  • Web games on their own hosts
  • Get your name, @id and avatar from OGS (signed for that game only)
  • Report a sitting label: "Mission 6", "Page 4 of 12", "Day 3"

First run

Make your OGS profile

One step: your name, an @id filled in from it, a sticker. One profile per device; a grown-up types the name on a kid's iPad. Back up with email (Apple and Google later), offered after onboarding, never required.

TV tab

The remote

The card on top mirrors the TV: the focused game's art and logo, its paused chip, and what OK will do. Someone who joined another person's TV sees "Leave Mom's TV" instead of Stop casting.

The TV launcher

The focused game fills the room

PS5-style: a row of game icons across the top, the focused game's art behind everything with its logo in the left third, and cards below. Text stays in the title-safe area; nothing covers the middle of a game.

Art

Every game has its own kit

Icon, cover with the title, transparent logo, and a clean hero with room for the logo. Made with Codex in each game's own look. No faces on objects.

Games inside OGS

Games know who you are

When you open a game, OGS hands it a token signed for that game only, with your profile. Rocket Crew skips "Who's playing here?" and seats you by name. Casting is OGS's job, so games show no cast buttons of their own.

A game sees

  • Profile id and @jonathan
  • Display name and sticker
  • On the TV: who's on the couch

A game never sees

  • Your friends list
  • Your other games
  • Device ids or push tokens
  • Your age

Beyond Chromecast

Platforms

The launcher runs directly where a device can run web (browsers, Fire TV, Google TV, Samsung, LG), and streams from the cloud over WebRTC where it can't (Chromecast today, Apple TV next). No phone rendering, no HLS, so Roku waits until it supports WebRTC. Detail: docs/adrs/2026-10-04-tv-platforms.md and docs/roadmap.md.
