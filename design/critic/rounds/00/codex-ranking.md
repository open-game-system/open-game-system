**Ranking**

1. **console**: Best match to the brief’s core pain. The TV is the anchor, the phone is plausibly the remote, kid iPads follow, game swapping is understandable, and Word Duel is handled without breaking the model. It needs brand elevation, not a model change.

2. **spine**: Strongest systems answer. The persistent bottom spine, saved-state cards, seat continuity, and swap progress are credible. But emotionally it is underpowered: too much “OS chrome,” not enough reason for a family to love OGS itself.

3. **kitchen**: Best family comprehension. “The Mumms,” devices, table setup, and grown-up-safe surfaces are humane. But Porchlight over-indexes on household hub. The evidence starts to look like a family organizer that also launches games, which is backwards for a game app.

4. **channel**: Best visual identity. The running order, idents, and TV graphics are memorable. But it forces every state into “on air / up next,” which breaks down for async turns, library browsing, and non-linear game nights.

5. **people**: Best async list. The person-thread model makes “your turn” obvious. But it is the wrong center of gravity: TV play, casting, game swapping, and kid following are all less natural than in console/spine.

**Brand Stance**

The evidence favors **OGS as a family game library/console**, with household identity as infrastructure. Not a general family hub, and not per-game apps. The winning stance is: the TV is the console, the grown-up phone is the remote/library, kid devices follow by identity.
